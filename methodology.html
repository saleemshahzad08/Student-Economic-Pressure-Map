Research Methodology & Empirical Machine Learning Framework

An Empirical, Transparent, and Responsible Study on Higher Education in Punjab, Pakistan.

Geographic Scope Punjab, Pakistan
Sample Size N = 514 Valid Respondents
Primary Metric Multiclass Macro F1
Primary Estimator Constrained Decision Tree (Entropy, Depth 4)

1. Research Context, Sampling & Instrument Design

Undergraduate and postgraduate students in developing economies face multidimensional socioeconomic strain that directly shapes academic progression, device accessibility, and career security[cite: 6]. Primary data was captured via a structured, cross-sectional questionnaire consisting of 15 standardized items across three functional domains[cite: 6]:

  • Domain A: Academic & Employment Profile (3 items): Captures employment status (work_status), work motivation (work_reason), and primary institutional expectations (education_improvement)[cite: 6].
  • Domain B: Technological Infrastructure & Perceptions (6 items): Likert-scale features assessing internet cost burden (internet_cost), device access (device_access), salary importance (salary_importance), job security, career confidence, and degree relevance[cite: 6].
  • Domain C: Economic Distress Indicators (6 items): Direct financial anxiety measures including academic distraction, emergency job worry, financial compromises, missed opportunities, family pressure, and career strain[cite: 6].

2. Target Construction (EPI) & Zero-Leakage Protocol

The 6 Domain C variables were scored on an integer scale (0 to 3), summed, and normalized to generate the continuous Economic Pressure Index (EPI)[cite: 6]:

EPI = ( ∑ Itemi / 18 ) × 100   [i = 1 to 6]

Across the reference sample (N = 514), the continuous EPI yielded a mean of 60.07 (±20.77), median of 61.11, and an IQR of 44.44 to 72.22[cite: 6]. To avoid subjective thresholding, the continuous index was discretized into three relative population tertiles[cite: 6]:

Low Pressure 0.00 ≤ EPI ≤ 55.56 43.77% (N = 225)
Moderate Pressure 55.56 < EPI ≤ 66.67 31.71% (N = 163)
High Pressure 66.67 < EPI ≤ 100.00 24.51% (N = 126)
Methodological Safeguard Against Target Leakage:

To ensure empirical integrity, all 6 Domain C distress variables used to construct the EPI were permanently removed from the feature matrix (X)[cite: 6]. The model estimates relative vulnerability strictly through external academic, technological, and behavioral predictors[cite: 6].

3. Feature Selection & Winning Predictor Set

Statistical association tests (Pearson's χ2 and Cramér's V) combined with out-of-fold Permutation Importance identified four core predictors[cite: 6]:

4. Constrained Model Architecture & Diagnostics

A white-box Decision Tree (criterion='entropy', max_depth=4, min_samples_leaf=25) was selected to provide transparent rule tracing without sacrificing predictive accuracy[cite: 6]. The minimum leaf constraint guarantees that every inferred rule represents at least 5% of the survey population (N ≥ 25)[cite: 6].

5. Responsible Governance & Privacy

  • Probabilistic Delivery: Hard classification boundaries can misrepresent borderline cases[cite: 6]. The frontend surfaces the full probability vector across all three tiers simultaneously[cite: 6].
  • Zero-Data Retention: Survey queries are evaluated entirely in memory over HTTPS and are discarded immediately after calculation[cite: 6]. No individual student records are logged or saved[cite: 6].
  • Non-Diagnostic Mandate: This system serves solely for institutional resource planning, equity audits, and pattern analysis[cite: 1, 6]. It is strictly prohibited from being used for clinical assessment or high-stakes gatekeeping[cite: 1, 6].

Privacy & Governance: Zero-Data Storage policy in effect. No personal student data is logged, stored, or transferred to third-party databases[cite: 1, 6].
